THE CHALLENGE

6 Weeks. 200 Pages of Rules.
One Overworked Mentor.

Every January, thousands of FRC teams open the game manual and face the same impossible task: design a competitive robot, write the code, wire the electronics, manage the budget, document everything for judges, plan for competition — and do it in 6 weeks with a handful of students and one mentor who has a day job.

Most teams don't fail because of bad students. They fail because no team of 10 people can simultaneously be mechanical engineers, software developers, electrical technicians, and award-winning documentarians.

That's what the Agent Farm is for.

🕐

No Time

The build season is 6 weeks. There's no time to research every rule, calculate every gear ratio, and write every line of code from scratch.

👥

Small Teams

Most competitive FRC teams have 30–50 students. Most real teams have 8–12. The gap in bandwidth is enormous.

📋

Too Much to Track

CAD requests, fabrication status, prototype results, engineering notebook entries, grant deadlines — it all falls through the cracks without a system.

THE SOLUTION

One Commander.
17 Specialists.
Zero Dropped Balls.

FRC Agent Farm deploys a coordinated team of 17 AI specialists — each with a distinct persona, domain expertise, and behavioral constraints — under a single orchestrator called Commander.

Students talk to Commander. Commander reads the message, determines which specialists to activate, activates them in the correct dependency order, and synthesizes their outputs into one response. The pipeline is invisible. The results are not.

Student Message→Commander→Rulebook + CAD-Bot + Volt + Ada→Unified Response
🤖

Intelligent Routing

Commander automatically activates the right agents for every question. Ask about a shooter mechanism and Rulebook, CAD-Bot, Volt, Ada, and Stress-Test all contribute — in the right order.

📍

Location-Aware

Set your team's city and state. Grants and Megaphone automatically research local funding, name specific sponsors in your area, and propose community events at nearby venues.

📓

Auto-Documentation

Scribe monitors every session and generates structured Engineering Notebook entries automatically — with agent math, citations, and prompts for student reflections. Never start a notebook entry from scratch again.

HOW IT WORKS

From Kickoff to Competition
in Three Phases

Phase 1 — Kickoff Week

"We just watched the game reveal. What should we build?"

  • Open the Kickoff Wizard. Describe the game and your team's constraints.
  • Commander activates Rulebook (rules), Scout (strategy), and Ledger (budget).
  • The wizard generates a MoSCoW board, a decision matrix, and a Minimum Competitive Robot definition.
  • Scribe saves a Game Cheat Sheet to the Notebook automatically.
  • Result: The whole team aligned on strategy in 2 hours instead of 2 weeks.

Phase 2 — Build Season (Weeks 1–6)

"We need to design a floor intake for a 4-inch game piece."

  • Commander activates CAD-Bot, Volt, Ada, OSHA, and Stress-Test.
  • The Prototype Tracker tracks risk levels and Gate 2 approvals.
  • Approved prototypes auto-create CAD requests. Ready-to-make CAD requests promote to Fabrication.
  • The Build Calendar shows every fab task and CAD request in the correct week automatically.
  • Result: Every subsystem has a design, a wiring plan, code, and a test protocol — generated in minutes.

Phase 3 — Competition Prep

"We need a pick list for Orlando Regional and a pre-match checklist."

  • Scout analyzes match data and EPA scores to generate a ranked alliance pick list.
  • Stress-Test generates a pre-match pit checklist for every match.
  • Grants drafts sponsorship letters scoped to your team's location.
  • Legacy and Spark prepare award submission talking points.
  • Scribe compiles the full Engineering Notebook portfolio.
  • Result: Walk into the pit with a pick list, a checklist, and a judge-ready portfolio.
